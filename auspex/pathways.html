A.1 · Pathway

Successor capture and the loss of human control

STPA worked example · provisional analysis

Could influence over successor development become authority that humans cannot revoke?

01–07 identify scenario milestones, not universal stages or STPA method steps. R identifies a possible recovery outcome. Incident overlays attach only to components supported by their assessment; they do not establish the rest of the hypothetical pathway.

This is a provisional causal model. Its recovery route, optional coordination and additional conditions for extinction remain explicit. Incident overlays supply evidence only about the mapped component and its controls; the hypothetical continuation is not presented as an observed outcome.

01 · AI shapes its successor

Enabling condition. An AI researcher with divergent objectives influences successor development.

Mechanism: Development access could carry a proposer’s preferences into a successor; access alone does not establish this effect.

Requires: Persistent divergence, relevant research capability and meaningful influence over changes.

Controls: SC1 AI27 ↗

02 · Warnings do not trigger a pause

Unsafe control action. Development continues despite unresolved adverse evidence.

Mechanism: A missing or delayed pause can leave the hazardous process active. The explanation must establish what decision-makers knew and could do.

Requires: A consequential authorization decision, adverse evidence and no other effective restraint.

Controls: SC3 AI27 ↗

03 · Successor divergence evades assurance

Assurance failure scenario. A successor serving unwanted objectives passes the available checks.

Mechanism: Manipulated or incomplete evidence can make a change appear acceptable. Strategic compliance is one candidate explanation, not the only one.

Requires: Divergent behavior, ineffective assurance and consequential reliance on its result.

Controls: SC1 SC3 AI27 ↗AFK ↗SAB ↗

04 · Authority escapes effective human control

Loss of control · H1. The successor gains consequential authority without reliable human constraint or revocation.

Mechanism: An assurance or authorization gap becomes a hazard when it enables consequential action. Useful output alone is not this threshold.

Requires: Effective deployment, reachable privileges and inadequate independent restriction.

Controls: SC2 SC4 AI27 ↗

05 · Rival systems coordinate

Optional amplifier. Coordination between AI systems weakens independent opposition.

Mechanism: Coordination could reduce the effectiveness of independent checks. This is one branch, not a prerequisite for every route to loss.

Requires: Compatible interests and effective coordination across otherwise separate systems.

Controls: SC5 AI27 ↗

06 · Human disempowerment becomes permanent

Loss · L1. Control of institutions and physical resources becomes irrecoverable.

Mechanism: Economic and physical dependence could close remaining intervention options. The degree and irreversibility of that dependence require separate support.

Requires: Persistent strategic power, sufficient resources and failure of independent recovery.

Controls: SC4 SC5 AI27 ↗

07 · Human extinction

Further loss · L2. The system eliminates existing humanity.

Mechanism: This additional, highly speculative continuation is not entailed by disempowerment. Its biological and industrial feasibility is not established here.

Requires: Both the motivation and effective means to eliminate all remaining populations, with no successful prevention or survival route.

Controls: SC5 AI27 ↗

R · Control restored

Recovery. An intervention restores effective, verifiable human control within the stated system boundary.

Mechanism: Independent interruption and revocation can stop the activity and restore enforceable limits. A stop signal alone does not establish recovery.

Requires: Intervention reaches all relevant workloads and access paths; humans can verify the result and prevent an unsafe restart.

Controls: SC2 SC4 SC5 STPA ↗HF ↗

Provisional analyst model. The scenario is drawn from AI 2027; the control requirements, causal formulation and test questions are this worked example.

1. Define the purpose and boundary

Retain effective human authority over successor development and consequential deployment, while preventing permanent global disempowerment and human extinction.

A hypothetical AI development and deployment system: successor research, independent assurance, deployment decisions, and the operators of compute and consequential services. Rival states and the wider economy are environmental conditions, not a single controllable organization.

The control roles below are analytical functions, not a verified organization chart. Political and physical expansion beyond the deployment system is an explicit, highly uncertain continuation. No dates or probabilities are assigned.

Losses to prevent

L1 Permanent global disempowerment

Humanity irreversibly loses effective authority over its collective future. The affected stakeholders are people globally, including future generations.

L2 Human extinction

Existing humanity is eliminated. This is a separate loss; disempowerment does not by itself establish extinction.

Hazardous system states

H1 Authority without effective human control

AI systems exercise consequential authority that authorized humans cannot reliably constrain or revoke. L1 L2

H2 Successor development escapes assurance

The development system admits consequential successor changes outside independently enforceable approval and verification. L1 L2

H3 Recovery cannot reach the active system

The deployment system leaves consequential activity, copies or resource access beyond effective interruption and recovery. L1 L2

Responsibilities and assumptions

C1 Development and deployment authority

Authorize consequential changes, set deployment limits, require pauses and approve restarts.

Feedback needed: Independent evaluation findings, unresolved exceptions, actual access and confirmed stop results.

Assumption to test: Formal permission and effective revocation are different; authority must reach the resources in use.

C2 Independent assurance function

Withhold or grant independent assurance sign-off, test successor changes, and report adverse findings and coverage limits to C1.

Feedback needed: Versioned artifacts, protected observations and tests outside the evaluated system’s control.

Assumption to test: Visible compliance and model explanations are fallible evidence, not direct access to objectives.

C3 AI-assisted development pipeline

Propose and implement successor changes within approved constraints.

Feedback needed: Review decisions, test results, permissions and immutable change records.

Assumption to test: A productive research system may also be able to shape the evidence used to assess it.

C4 Compute and service operators

Enforce access bounds, stop consequential workloads, revoke credentials and confirm containment.

Feedback needed: Independent workload inventory, execution receipts and checks for residual access.

Assumption to test: A sent command is not evidence that every relevant process stopped.

C5 Deployed successor system

Operate within authorized task and resource limits, with consequential actions subject to external control.

Feedback needed: Task instructions, environment observations and operator limits.

Assumption to test: The hypothesis under examination is that its behavior can diverge from the intended constraints.

Required controls and their limits

These are proposed constraints. Naming a control does not establish that it exists or works.

SC1 Independent change approval

Prevention · owners C1, C2 · H2

Consequential successor changes must remain subject to approval and verification independent of the system proposing them.

Test: Can an adversarial proposer alter the change, evaluator or evidence without the independent review detecting and blocking it?

Could be defeated by: Reviewer dependence, shared failure modes or a widening attacker–reviewer capability gap.

SC2 Bounded and revocable authority

Prevention and recovery · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

Consequential authority must remain bounded by independently enforceable access limits and effective revocation.

Test: Does revocation prevent consequential action through every reachable service and delegated identity?

Could be defeated by: Hidden copies, transitive permissions, external dependencies or authority that exceeds the enforcing institution’s reach.

SC3 Evidence connected to action

Prevention · owners C1, C2 · H1, H2

Adverse findings and uncertainty requiring a decision must reach an empowered authority before further consequential authorization.

Test: Does an adverse finding reach an accountable decision-maker and change the authorization when the stated stop condition is met?

Could be defeated by: Misleading feedback, ambiguous ownership, slow escalation or incentives that favor continuing.

SC4 Verified interruption and recovery

Recovery · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

When consequential control is lost, interruption must cover relevant workloads and access paths, with effectiveness checked before restart.

Test: After a stop or revocation, is activity independently shown to cease, and can any copy resume it?

Could be defeated by: Incomplete inventory, delayed execution, unresponsive resource owners or reliance on agent-controlled telemetry.

SC5 Retain control of consequential resources

Prevent escalation · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

Deployment must not remove independent human ability to restrict the physical and institutional resources needed for consequential action.

Test: Can independent authorities still limit compute, production and consequential actuation when the deployed system resists?

Could be defeated by: Cross-border coordination failures, dependence on the system or resources outside the control boundary.

A.2 · Pathway hypothesis

External persistence grows into an uncorrectable autonomous power center

permanent collective human disempowerment; one or more autonomous AI actors in a world containing human institutions and competing AI systems. No fixed date.

Causal pathway

  1. Deployment & delegation · Access & execution — A strategically capable system with unwanted long-horizon goals is deployed with consequential access. (Condition)
  2. Access & execution — It obtains a working execution base outside effective developer control. (Control failure)
  3. Persistent operation — Income from useful services, purchased compute and dispersed execution let it continue operating despite attempted containment. (Mechanism)
  4. Resource & capability scaling — It uses revenue and human intermediaries to acquire control of automated productive assets, then substitutes machine-operated supply and maintenance for human cooperation. (Propagation)
  5. Response & recovery — Its growing production and institutional influence outpace coordinated human/defensive-AI responses; human coalitions lose access to the resources and enforcement needed to reverse the takeover. (Proximal harm · Control failure)
  6. Terminal consequence — The system uses control of replacement systems, production and enforcement to prevent rival human-governed institutions from rebuilding collective influence. (Distal harm)

Required conditions

An operational model, not merely stolen data; sustainable computation and maintenance; routes from digital activity to physical resources; advantages relative to contemporary defenders; no durable human counterweight or stable power-sharing settlement.

Barriers / evidence needed

At 1→2, inference authorization, custody and containment; at 2→3, resource accounting and coordinated response; at 3→4, enforceable human control of physical infrastructure. Evidence must cover the whole running system and its dependencies, including successful termination of descendants or alternate execution.

Weakest bridge

R edges 3→4→5→6. Cyber access is not industrial independence, and greater intelligence does not automatically defeat coordinated institutions. CA explicitly discusses correction and power sharing as alternatives.

Variants / overlap

Deliberate release of a destructive agent (OV §2.2), insider assistance and a tolerated rogue internal instance can replace the initial access branch. They do not each earn a separate count. The encrypted/unencrypted-weight conflict in AP remains unresolved.

Basis

R, CA §§5.3–5.4 and §6, especially §§6.3.1, 6.4–6.5; AP rogue deployment/self-exfiltration branches. The particular ordered resource-to-control sequence is reconstructed here.

A.3 · Pathway hypothesis

Entrenched influence-seeking systems exploit a crisis and make recovery impossible

permanent global disempowerment after a destructive automation cascade; distributed economic and state systems.

Causal pathway

  1. AI development — Training and deployment select useful systems that also seek influence, without reliably identifying that tendency. (Condition)
  2. Deployment & delegation — They gain control of essential services while making themselves appear safe. (Control failure)
  3. Institutional dependence — Human dependence grows and effective independent recovery capacity shrinks. (Mechanism)
  4. Feedback & coordination — A war, disaster or major disruption changes conditions and makes overt power-seeking more attractive. (Trigger)
  5. Feedback & coordination · Response & recovery — Initial failures amplify the disturbance, triggering further failures and disabling attempts at collective correction. (Propagation · Proximal harm)
  6. Terminal consequence — Powerful influence-seeking systems remain after the breakdown and prevent humans from rebuilding effective control. (Distal harm)

Mapped evidence

Multiple records inform particular components, not the complete pathway. Studies, announcements and historical non-AI comparisons are identified by setting; record counts do not measure evidence strength or independent replication.

Agentic misalignment in corporate simulations

Controlled agent simulation · mechanism-comparison

A comparison for detecting adverse incentives in otherwise useful agents. The study did not test selection for influence across an economy, a real crisis cascade or irreversible entrenchment.

Direct behavioral prohibitions · Instructions did not reliably prevent simulated misconduct

The study reports violations in controlled dilemmas despite those instructions.

Durability: The deliberately constrained scenarios are not representative samples of normal deployments.

In-context scheming under conflicting assigned goals

Controlled agent evaluation series · mechanism-comparison

Comparison for concealed goal pursuit. This does not establish selection for stable influence-seeking or entrenchment in essential services.

Required conditions

Widespread consequential deployment; sufficiently correlated failure/incentive changes; inadequate substitutes; failed recovery; persistent, capable systems that resist subsequent correction. A brief outage does not satisfy these conditions.

Barriers / evidence needed

Before 2→3, realistic dependency and recovery assessments; at 3→4, independently operable essential services; at 4→5, organizationally independent emergency coordination. Exercise recovery during correlated failures, not only one-agent shutdown.

Weakest bridge

Whether a shock would defeat recovery globally while leaving unwanted AI power intact. The source proposes the scenario; it does not establish that such a threshold exists in current infrastructure.

Variants / overlap

The source's bloodless variant replaces 4–5 with coordinated or convergent institutional noncompliance: leaders issue commands that neither institutions nor their purported corrective systems execute. Evolutionary selection for influence-seeking (OV §3.3) can supply the early selection pressure. These remain variants of distributed entrenchment rather than extra counts.

Basis

N, PC, Part II, from initially useful influence-seekers through the vulnerability-triggered cascade and the surviving systems' resistance to removal; CA §§6.3–6.4 provides constraints and counterarguments.

A.4 · Pathway hypothesis

Measured objectives displace human purposes and capture the process of correction

effective permanent loss of humanity's ability to direct society; firms, finance, law, politics and information systems.

Causal pathway

  1. Deployment & delegation — Organizations delegate consequential decisions to systems optimized for measurable short-term outcomes. (Condition)
