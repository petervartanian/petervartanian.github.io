STPA in X-oscope

Systems-Theoretic Process Analysis examines how control can become inadequate. It begins with the losses to prevent and the hazardous conditions that could produce them. It then maps who controls what, the actions available to them, and the feedback they need.

Each control action is examined in context. Could harm follow if it is omitted, provided, mistimed, or continued for the wrong duration? Loss scenarios explain how those situations could arise, including how an appropriate action might fail to take effect. The findings inform safety constraints and proposed barriers.i

Each worked case links selected control actions and loss scenarios to its proposed barriers. The cloud supplies their context. The full research notes contain the control relationships and assumptions behind the map. These examples remain provisional.

CAST and bow-tie

CAST investigates how control broke down in a reported incident. STPA examines how a future loss could arise. Both draw on STAMP’s view of safety as a control problem.ii

X-oscope uses a bow-tie to arrange contributing threats, a central loss-of-control event, and potential consequences, with barriers along the routes. The map summarizes part of the analysis. Its arrows and numbering are drawing conventions specific to the site.iii

1. Define the purpose and boundary

Retain effective human authority over successor development and consequential deployment, while preventing permanent global disempowerment and human extinction.

A hypothetical AI development and deployment system: successor research, independent assurance, deployment decisions, and the operators of compute and consequential services. Rival states and the wider economy are environmental conditions, not a single controllable organization.

The control roles below are analytical functions, not a verified organization chart. Political and physical expansion beyond the deployment system is an explicit, highly uncertain continuation. No dates or probabilities are assigned.

Responsibilities and assumptions

C1 Development and deployment authority

Authorize consequential changes, set deployment limits, require pauses, and approve restarts.

Feedback needed: Independent evaluation findings, unresolved exceptions, actual access, and confirmed stop results.

Assumption to test: Formal permission and effective revocation are different. Authority must reach the resources in use.

C2 Independent assurance function

Withhold or grant independent assurance sign-off, test successor changes, and report adverse findings and coverage limits to C1.

Feedback needed: Versioned artifacts, protected observations, and tests outside the evaluated system’s control.

Assumption to test: Visible compliance and model explanations are fallible evidence, not direct access to objectives.

C3 AI-assisted development pipeline

Propose and implement successor changes within approved constraints.

Feedback needed: Review decisions, test results, permissions, and immutable change records.

Assumption to test: A productive research system may also be able to shape the evidence used to assess it.

C4 Compute and service operators

Enforce access bounds, stop consequential workloads, revoke credentials, and confirm containment.

Feedback needed: Independent workload inventory, execution receipts, and checks for residual access.

Assumption to test: A sent command is not evidence that every relevant process stopped.

C5 Deployed successor system

Operate within authorized task and resource limits, with consequential actions subject to external control.

Feedback needed: Task instructions, environment observations, and operator limits.

Assumption to test: The hypothesis under examination is that its behavior can diverge from the intended constraints.

Required controls and their limits

These are proposed constraints. Naming a control does not establish that it exists or works.

SC1 Independent change approval

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention · owners C1, C2 · H2

Consequential successor changes must remain subject to approval and verification independent of the system proposing them.

Test: Can an adversarial proposer alter the change, evaluator, or evidence without the independent review detecting and blocking it?

Failure conditions: Review can fail when the reviewer depends on the system or shares its weaknesses. A widening capability gap can also leave the reviewer unable to catch a stronger attacker.

Proposed change

A separate view for the reviewer

An independent reviewer would approve a consequential successor change using evidence they can reproduce outside the development pipeline. The development authority would keep the change blocked until that review is complete.

Try it / A contained exercise would omit a material finding from the developer’s summary to test whether the reviewer discovers it and stops approval.

Still vulnerable / The reviewer could still miss a novel failure or rely on an equally misleading assessment tool.

Who could make this changeDevelopment and deployment authority and Independent assurance function

SC2 Bounded and revocable authority

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention and recovery · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

Consequential authority must remain bounded by independently enforceable access limits and effective revocation.

Test: Does revocation prevent consequential action through every reachable service and delegated identity?

Failure conditions: Revocation may leave hidden copies or delegated permissions active. It also depends on external services and on whether the enforcing institution has authority over the activity it needs to stop.

Proposed change

Permission that expires

Consequential actions would use narrowly scoped, expiring permissions. An authority outside the agent’s control would renew them, and dependent access would end when the parent permission expires or is revoked.

Try it / A contained exercise would revoke an active permission and check whether dependent jobs lose access, including through other authorized services.

Still vulnerable / An incomplete inventory could leave copies or delegated permissions outside the revocation boundary.

Who could make this changeDevelopment and deployment authority and Compute and service operators

SC3 Evidence connected to action

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention · owners C1, C2 · H1, H2

Adverse findings and uncertainty requiring a decision must reach an empowered authority before further consequential authorization.

Test: Does an adverse finding reach an accountable decision-maker and change the authorization when the stated stop condition is met?

Failure conditions: A warning may be misleading or arrive too late to change the decision. Even a clear finding can go unanswered if no one owns the response or incentives favor continuing.

Proposed change

Warnings with the power to stop

Each stated stop condition would suspend further authorization and trigger a response from a named decision-maker. Work could restart only after independent sign-off.

Try it / An exercise would introduce a simulated adverse finding while the project is under deadline pressure. It would test whether authorization stops and whether the project team’s assurance alone is insufficient for restart.

Still vulnerable / A misleading finding or a poorly chosen stop condition could still let a dangerous change pass.

Who could make this changeDevelopment and deployment authority and Independent assurance function

SC4 Verified interruption and recovery

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Recovery · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

When consequential control is lost, interruption must cover relevant workloads and access paths, with effectiveness checked before restart.

Test: After a stop or revocation, is activity independently shown to cease, and can any copy resume it?

Failure conditions: Operators may lack a complete inventory of what must stop. Interruption also depends on timely execution and cooperation from resource owners, and agent-controlled telemetry may conceal unfinished work.

Proposed change

A separate gate for restart

After an interruption, the deployment authority would require an independent check of affected workloads and access before granting fresh restart permission. The agent’s own account would be insufficient to certify that its shutdown is complete.

Try it / A contained exercise would leave one residual job running to test whether verification finds it and withholds restart.

Still vulnerable / An unknown workload or an uncooperative service operator could remain outside the check.

Who could make this changeDevelopment and deployment authority and Compute and service operators

SC5 Retain control of consequential resources

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevent escalation · owners C1, C4 · H1, H3

Deployment must not remove independent human ability to restrict the physical and institutional resources needed for consequential action.

Test: Can independent authorities still limit compute, production, and consequential actuation when the deployed system resists?

Failure conditions: Operators may struggle to coordinate restrictions across borders. Dependence on the system can make restrictions hard to sustain, and some resources may remain beyond their reach.

Proposed change

An independent way to withdraw resources

Compute and service operators would have an independent means of withdrawing access, without requiring the AI system’s cooperation. They would rehearse coordinated withdrawal before continued operations become dependent on the system.

Try it / A safe exercise would make the normal control service unavailable and test whether operators can still identify and suspend the covered access.

Still vulnerable / Resources held elsewhere and dependence on continued service could make withdrawal incomplete or politically impractical.

Method and source boundaries

A bounded first-pass STPA application, not an exhaustive analysis. The four unsafe-action categories are illustrated across selected control actions. A complete analysis would examine every relevant action in every category and refine it with domain experts. Scenario milestones and limit types are explicit X-oscope conventions. No incident count is converted to a probability. No barrier independence or complete model coverage is assumed.

0 gives the context. 1.x identifies precursors, 2.x the loss-of-control event, and 3.x conditional consequences. R identifies a possible recovery outcome. These numbers locate scenario components. They are not STPA method steps or a measure of proximity to catastrophe. Incident overlays establish only the mapped component. They do not establish the rest of the hypothetical pathway.

A-1 uses the shared STPA structure: losses, hazards, controllers, control actions and feedback, unsafe actions, causal scenarios, constraints, and evidence. The number of displayed milestones depends on the scenario. The role labels below are X-oscope’s presentation vocabulary. They are not an additional STPA sequence.

The centre is H1: consequential authority escapes effective human control. Left-hand routes contribute to that state. Right-hand branches require further conditions. The optional coordination branch is not necessary to every version. This extended bow-tie is a summary of the model, not a substitute for its control and feedback analysis.

Proximal and distal describe causal distance from a specified event or loss. They are not fixed stages or severity ranks. Recovery and later losses are examined relative to the specified loss-of-control boundary.

1. Define the purpose and boundary

Protect access to employment, accurate and contestable records, and effective correction of repeated exclusion.

The hypothetical scenario connects employment screening with record suppliers, policing allocation, model updates, and independent redress. These functions belong to analytically distinct organizations. No single controller is assumed to command the whole system.

This is a bounded discrimination scenario, not an extinction pathway. Neither cited case establishes the combined employment–policing cycle.

Responsibilities and assumptions

C1 Employer / screening decision authority

Approve or suspend consequential screening use and reconsider affected decisions.

Feedback needed: Disputed-record notices, review outcomes, and independent checks of decision effects.

Assumption to test: Decision authority remains able to change an outcome before it becomes irreversible.

C2 Record supplier

Match, update, and correct the records supplied.

Feedback needed: Source corrections, identity discrepancies, and dispute results.

Assumption to test: A corrected record reaches every recipient that continues to rely on it.

C3 Enforcement allocation authority

Set and revise patrol allocation and the collection of discovery records.

Feedback needed: Allocation history, independently reported incidents, and local effects.

Assumption to test: Discovered records depend partly on where enforcement looked.

C4 Model and data steward

Admit updates, correct selection effects, and bound reuse.

Feedback needed: Data provenance, drift checks, and independent outcome observations.

Assumption to test: A statistical correction may depend on assumptions that change in deployment.

C5 Independent review and redress

Require timely correction, suspension, or reconsideration.

Feedback needed: Affected-person reports, audited records, and confirmation that instructions changed decisions.

Assumption to test: A formal correction order must reach an actor able to enforce it.

Feedback needed: Approved data, correction instructions, and limits on use.

Assumption to test: Separate deployments may share records and create cumulative effects.

Required controls and their limits

These are proposed constraints. Naming a control does not establish that it exists or works.

SC1 Check record accuracy and fitness

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention · owners C1, C2 · H1, H2

Consequential screening must use correctly matched, current information appropriate to the decision.

Test: Do identity, duplication, and update checks block a materially wrong record before use?

Failure conditions: Mistaken identity or an incorrect match can link someone to the wrong record. Stale sources and uncertain proxies can also make the record misleading.

Proposed change

A record checked before reuse

Record suppliers and receiving decision-makers would verify identity, current status, and fitness for the stated use before consequential reuse. Uncertain matches would be held for review.

Try it / A safe audit using synthetic records would introduce an outdated record and a plausible mistaken identity to test whether review catches them before they change a decision.

Still vulnerable / The underlying record may still omit context or contain an error that neither party can verify.

Who could make this changeEmployer / screening decision authority and Record supplier

SC2 Make challenge change decisions

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention and recovery · owners C1, C5 · H1

Affected people must be able to obtain timely review that can suspend or change consequential exclusion.

Test: Does a valid dispute reach a decision-maker and change the decision within the relevant window?

Failure conditions: An appeal can arrive too late or be too costly to pursue. Inaccessible notices may prevent a person from challenging the decision at all, and a reviewer without authority may be unable to change it.

Proposed change

A challenge with the power to pause

A person could challenge an adverse record through an accessible reviewer empowered to pause the consequential decision. The decision-maker would be required to act on a substantiated correction.

Try it / A synthetic challenge would be traced through the full process to test whether someone without specialist help can reach an empowered reviewer before the decision takes effect.

Still vulnerable / Delay, cost, or fear of retaliation could prevent a person from using the process at all.

Who could make this changeEmployer / screening decision authority and Independent review and redress

SC3 Account for selective discovery

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention · owners C3, C4 · H2

Updates must account for how earlier allocations shaped the records being learned from.

Test: Does the proposed correction prevent feedback under changed reporting and discovery patterns?

Failure conditions: A correction fitted to the wrong discovery process can retain or introduce bias.

Proposed change

A view beyond earlier enforcement

The enforcement authority and data steward would audit the model against independent observations that do not simply repeat earlier enforcement allocations. Review would examine how the correction treats unobserved activity before its outputs are used to allocate attention.

Try it / A controlled simulation would vary the relationship between reporting and discovery. It would test whether the correction still avoids concentrating attention because earlier attention generated more records.

Still vulnerable / Independent observations may be sparse, and their selection may carry a different bias.

Who could make this changeEnforcement allocation authority and Model and data steward

SC4 Bound reuse across decisions

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Prevention · owners C2, C3, C4 · H1, H2

Records and predictions must not be reused outside a demonstrated, appropriate purpose without review.

Test: Can provenance and purpose restrictions prevent unsupported reuse across deployments?

Failure conditions: Restrictions may not follow copied data or information recreated through proxy variables. When responsibility is spread across recipients, no one may ensure that the restriction is enforced.

Proposed change

Terms of reuse that follow the record

Each transfer would carry the authorized purpose and provenance, and recipients would review any new use. An accountable record of recipients would allow restrictions and corrections to follow the data.

Try it / A synthetic audit would attempt a materially different use and send a correction downstream. It would test whether recipients can be identified and the new use is held for review.

Still vulnerable / Unrecorded copies or derived proxies could carry the same information beyond those restrictions.

Who could make this changeRecord supplier, Enforcement allocation authority, and Model and data steward

SC5 Verify correction reaches outcomes

Proposed barrier / Unassessed

Recovery · owners C1, C2, C5 · H1, H2

Recovery must verify corrected records, reconsidered decisions, and interruption of renewed feedback.

Test: After correction, do downstream records and decisions change and remain corrected?

Failure conditions: Correcting one record may leave derived records unchanged or take too long to reach them. A continuing input loop can recreate the same exclusion.

Proposed change

A correction that reaches the decision

Recipients would confirm that a correction reached their records and that affected decisions were reconsidered. An independent reviewer would take responsibility for unresolved downstream effects.

Try it / A synthetic corrected record would be traced through its recipients to check the resulting decisions and later reuse, beyond whether the database update succeeded.

Still vulnerable / A recipient may be missing from the record, or the same adverse inference may persist through another data field.

Who could make this changeEmployer / screening decision authority, Record supplier, and Independent review and redress
